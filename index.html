How to use

  1. Tap Check in and talk for 30–60 seconds about anything. Don't read aloud.
  2. While it analyzes, rate how you feel and tap where you feel it in your body (optional). Add a quick note if you like; over time your notes become a short journal alongside your scores.
  3. Read your result. What might help offers grounding and recovery ideas. Pick one, or none.
  4. Check in regularly. After 5 check-ins, you'll start to see how each day compares with your usual.
  5. Private by design: your recording is never saved; it's thrown away after scoring. Everything stays on your device and works fully offline. You can export your history as a backup, to move it to a new device, or to share with a health professional. Because it's personal health information, we don't recommend sharing it with anyone else.

Why regular check-ins help: noticing where you feel something and putting a name to it builds the skill of reading your own signals. That can be especially helpful for people with alexithymia, who find it hard to identify what they feel.

What this is

A private way to notice patterns in how you're doing. Each check-in records 30 to 60 seconds of you talking, then a voice model listens to how you speak (pitch, rhythm, energy, pauses), not what you say. It gives two signals: anxiety and low mood.

It's also a practice. Describing what's around you is grounding. Talking about your day is reflection. Rating yourself before you see the result, and noticing where you feel things in your body, builds the skill of reading your own signals. That may help people with alexithymia, who find it hard to name what they feel.

How accurate is it?

In Kintsugi's published study of about 15,000 adults (Annals of Family Medicine, 2025), the voice model:

  • missed about 3 in 10 people who were depressed (read them as not depressed), and
  • wrongly flagged about 3 in 10 people who weren't.
  • When it flagged someone, about 7 in 10 really did score as depressed on the questionnaire, in a group where nearly half were.
  • These numbers come from an earlier yes/no version of the depression model. No published accuracy exists yet for the severity levels, or for anxiety.
  • A separate independent study found similar, modest accuracy, and worse results for some groups (for example, pregnant women).
  • It was trained mostly on American English speech. Other languages and accents may be less accurate.
  • Levels line up with two standard questionnaires: the PHQ-9 (depression) and the GAD-7 (anxiety).

That's why this app is built for tracking changes in you, not for diagnosis. Your own readings over weeks show your pattern. A single reading can be wrong in either direction. If you feel worse than your voice reads, trust what you feel.

This is a personal tracking and research tool, not a medical device. Diagnosis and treatment need a doctor or licensed clinician.

Privacy

Your recording is analyzed on this device and then thrown away. Only the scores, your ratings, and notes are saved, in this app only. Nothing is sent anywhere. The model was downloaded once from this site, and the app checks its fingerprints.

See for yourself: turn on airplane mode and do a check-in. It still works with no internet, because nothing is sent anywhere.

To keep a copy of your history, use Export a copy at the top of the History tab.

With gratitude to Kintsugi Health

This app runs the DAM 3.1 Depression and Anxiety Model made by Kintsugi Health. They spent years and real resources building and testing it, worked toward FDA review, and then released it openly so it wouldn't go to waste. Thank you to the whole team and to the thousands of people whose voices made it possible.

Model: KintsugiHealth/dam, Apache License 2.0. Converted to half-precision ONNX for on-device use (scores match the original within 0.001). Built on OpenAI's Whisper-small.en. Runs with ONNX Runtime Web (MIT License).

The body map is inspired by research on where people feel emotions in their bodies, by Lauri Nummenmaa and colleagues (PNAS, 2014 and 2018). The drawing, feeling lists, and suggestions are this app's own simple version, not a clinical tool.
